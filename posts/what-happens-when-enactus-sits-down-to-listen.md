# What Happens when Enactus Sits Down to Listen

## ***<span style="font-size: 13pt; line-height: 19.9333px;"> </span>******<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">“Never let your circumstances decide who you are.”</span>***<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;"> These words are often linked to Arunima Sinha, the first female amputee to climb Mount Everest.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">Her journey is proof that with the right support, strong will power, and determination, anyone can overcome great odds. A similar message can be seen in the lives of Steve Jobs, who had dyslexia and faced many failures, and Helen Keller, who could neither see nor hear yet went on to write several books. Their stories remind us that people grow stronger when someone listens to them and believes in them. This is where the heart of Enactus lies. We aim to create spaces where people feel heard and supported, so that they can move forward with confidence.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">With this belief, Enactus Hansraj took a step toward mental health under Armaan, a vertical of Project Ahsaas. Our aim was not to deliver a lecture, but to listen. This is also the first initiative of its kind that our team has undertaken in the last 2-3 years, and we approached it with great enthusiasm and a strong sense of responsibility.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">On 24th October, 2026, at 11:00 AM, the Enactus team visited the Deep Blind Welfare Association, Delhi . The objective of the session was to interact, listen, and motivate through shared stories and open conversation, as part of our social outreach initiative.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">The session was facilitated by Dr. Aditya Vohra, a psychiatrist who engages people through stories and honest dialogue. Ms. Nirmal, the NGO’s coordinator, supported us throughout. We were joined by 12 members of the Association, whose openness and warmth made the entire session meaningful.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">From the very first introduction, the room felt comfortable and welcoming. Members spoke about their daily challenges, their dreams, and the way they have faced society’s judgement. What we gained from these conversations was far more than what we had expected to give.</span>

<span style="font-size: medium;"></span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">There was no stage and no lecture. Instead the room held an open comfortable circle in which people felt free to speak. When Dr. Vohra asked the members about the difficulties they face in daily life, they answered honestly and that candour built the trust on which the rest of the session rested.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;"> </span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">The heart of the day was storytelling and one story stood out above the rest that is of Shrikant Bolla. Born visually impaired, Shrikant went on to fight a system that was not designed with him in mind. He challenged barriers in education and opportunity, refused to accept limitations placed on him by others and ultimately built a company of his own. His journey  which has also inspired a feature film, was presented not as a tale of exceptional luck but as one of determination and courage with clarity of purpose.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;"> </span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">For an audience of visually impaired members, the story carried particular weight. Shrikant was not a distant figure from a different world hiis struggles were their struggles and his success showed what a determined mindset can achieve. The message Dr. Vohra drew from it was simple and powerful: everyone has problems, yours are merely more visible to others, and life should be taken as a challenge to be met rather than a complication to be endured. How we respond to a problem matters more than the problem itself.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;"> </span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">The conversation that followed gave this message a personal meaning. Members spoke openly about their dreams and aspirations and about the harsh judgements society has passed on them. Just as importantly, they shared how they overcame those moments. The session closed with an appreciation activity in which each person said something kind about the one sitting beside them leaving the room with a sense of togetherness and positivity.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;"> </span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">None of this would have been possible without the dedicated efforts of Enactus Hansraj. Directly the team collaborated with the Deep Blind Welfare Association and brought in Dr. Vohra. Team members were present throughout, not as spectators but as active listeners and participants.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;"> </span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">Indirectly, the team's contribution lay in the quieter work: coordinating with Ms. Nirmal, the NGO's coordinator, planning the flow of the session and most importantly designing it as a conversation rather than a lecture. By choosing to listen first Enactus created a safe space where members could speak without fear of judgement. This approach reflects the organisation's belief that real social impact begins with empathy and respect.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;"> </span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">This session also holds special significance for the society itself. It marks the revival of such outreach initiatives by Enactus Hansraj after a gap of three years. Their return to direct community based engagement signals a renewed commitment to the values on which the society was built.</span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;"> </span>

<span style="font-size: 12pt; line-height: 18.4px;">As the day ended one thing was clear: sometimes the most meaningful form of support is not a solution handed down but a story shared, a voice heard and a space made. Enactus Hansraj hopes this is only the beginning of many such conversations to come.</span>
