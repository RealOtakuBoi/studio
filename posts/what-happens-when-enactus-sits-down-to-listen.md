> “Never let your circumstances decide who you are.”

These words are often linked to Arunima Sinha, the first female amputee to climb Mount Everest. Her journey shows that with support, determination, and strong will, people can overcome great odds. A similar message can be found in the lives of Steve Jobs, who had dyslexia and faced many failures, and Helen Keller, who could neither see nor hear yet went on to write several books. Their stories remind us that people grow stronger when someone listens to them and believes in them. This is where the heart of Enactus lies: creating spaces where people feel heard and supported so they can move forward with confidence.

With this belief, Enactus Hansraj took a step toward mental health through Armaan, a vertical of Project Ahsaas. Our aim was not to deliver a lecture, but to listen. This was also the first initiative of its kind our team had undertaken in the last two to three years, and we approached it with enthusiasm and a strong sense of responsibility.

On 24 October 2026, at 11:00 AM, the Enactus team visited the Deep Blind Welfare Association in Delhi. The objective of the session was to interact, listen, and motivate through shared stories and open conversation as part of our social outreach initiative.

The session was facilitated by Dr. Aditya Vohra, a psychiatrist who engages people through stories and honest dialogue. Ms. Nirmal, the NGO's coordinator, supported us throughout. We were joined by 12 members of the Association, whose openness and warmth made the session meaningful.

From the very first introduction, the room felt comfortable and welcoming. Members spoke about their daily challenges, their dreams, and the ways they had faced society's judgment. What we gained from these conversations was far more than we had expected to give.

There was no stage and no lecture. Instead, the room was arranged in an open circle where people felt free to speak. When Dr. Vohra asked members about the difficulties they face in daily life, they answered honestly. Their candor built the trust on which the rest of the session rested.

The heart of the day was storytelling, and one story stood out: that of Shrikant Bolla. Born visually impaired, Shrikant went on to challenge a system that was not designed with him in mind. He overcame barriers in education and opportunity, refused to accept limitations placed on him by others, and ultimately built a company of his own. His journey, which also inspired a feature film, was presented not as a tale of exceptional luck, but as one of determination, courage, and clarity of purpose.

For an audience of visually impaired members, the story carried particular weight. Shrikant was not a distant figure from another world; his struggles resonated with theirs, and his success showed what a determined mindset can achieve. The message Dr. Vohra drew from it was simple and powerful: everyone has problems, but some are more visible to others. Life should be taken as a challenge to meet rather than a complication to endure. How we respond to a problem matters more than the problem itself.

The conversation that followed gave this message personal meaning. Members spoke openly about their dreams and aspirations, as well as the harsh judgments society had passed on them. Just as importantly, they shared how they had overcome those moments. The session closed with an appreciation activity in which each person said something kind about the person sitting beside them. Everyone left with a sense of togetherness and positivity.

None of this would have been possible without the dedicated efforts of Enactus Hansraj. The team collaborated with the Deep Blind Welfare Association and brought in Dr. Vohra. Team members were present throughout, not as spectators but as active listeners and participants.

The team's contribution also included quieter work: coordinating with Ms. Nirmal, planning the flow of the session, and, most importantly, designing it as a conversation rather than a lecture. By choosing to listen first, Enactus created a welcoming space where members could speak without fear of judgment. This approach reflects the organisation's belief that real social impact begins with empathy and respect.

This session also holds special significance for the society itself. It marks the revival of Enactus Hansraj's outreach initiatives after a gap of three years. The return to direct, community-based engagement signals a renewed commitment to the values on which the society was built.

As the day ended, one thing was clear: sometimes the most meaningful form of support is not a solution handed down, but a story shared, a voice heard, and a space made. Enactus Hansraj hopes this is only the beginning of many such conversations to come.
